\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Two snapshots of a growing population hours apart hold more lineage than trackers extract, even fine-tuned at the gap.
Registering families, by moving one centre per ancestor rather than matching cells, recovers most of it at short and medium gaps, without annotation: simulation transfers across colonies, binary fission turns unannotated time-lapses into near-annotation labels, and one image reveals close relatives.
What limits long gaps is telling neighbouring families apart.
