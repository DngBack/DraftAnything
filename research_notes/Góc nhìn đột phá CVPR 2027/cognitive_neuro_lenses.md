# Cross-disciplinary lenses (cognitive science, neuroscience, psychophysics, physics) for a CVPR 2027 paper

Scope note: today is 2026-10-13; the CVPR 2027 deadline is 2026-11-16, about 34 days away, with one H200. Ideas already in `reports/` are excluded or marked as overlaps. Those are the point-light V-JEPA probe, MLLM "arbitration", zoom as a resolution knob, GUI patch-phase aliasing, SAM memory error propagation, the 4D physics judge, VLA modality attribution, and world-model evaluators ranked against LIBERO. All GPU-day figures are **my own estimates** and have not been measured. Sources marked "(background, not re-fetched)" are well-known papers cited from prior knowledge. Their URLs are standard arXiv IDs, but I did not re-open them in this session.

## Q1. Which cross-disciplinary borrowings had outsized impact (2020–2026), and which got orals or best papers?

### Takeaway
The borrowings that lasted turned a concept into a **measurable quantity or objective**. Examples are free-energy/score matching becoming the diffusion loss, prediction error in a latent space becoming the JEPA loss and a "surprise" readout, and the shape/texture cue-conflict becoming a bias score. Borrowings that stayed metaphors, such as "System 1/2" or "brain-inspired" modules, produced popular architectures but few top awards. I found **no CVPR or ICCV main-conference best paper in 2024–2026 that is framed as cognitively inspired**. The cognitively framed work that won recognition came through ML venues (ICLR/NeurIPS), high-impact journals (Science, Nature MI), or honorable mentions (Navigation World Models).

### Cited Findings
**Catalogue of borrowings (concept → CV/ML work → status):**
- **Statistical physics / non-equilibrium thermodynamics → diffusion.** Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015 explicitly borrowed non-equilibrium thermodynamics. DDPM (NeurIPS 2020) and score-SDE (ICLR 2021, Outstanding Paper Award) made it the dominant generative paradigm — [Sohl-Dickstein 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03585), [DDPM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239), [Score SDE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456) (background, not re-fetched; ICLR 2021 award is from my memory).
- **Hopfield networks / associative memory ↔ attention.** "Hopfield Networks is All You Need" (ICLR 2021) showed that the modern continuous Hopfield update equals transformer attention — [arXiv 2008.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217) (background). The same CLS-style Hopfield-as-hippocampus idea is still being used in 2025 continual-learning models, but only on Split-MNIST — [arXiv 2507.11393](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11393).
- **Predictive coding / prediction in latent space → JEPA.** I-JEPA (CVPR 2023) and V-JEPA 2 (2025) — [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243), [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985) (background). The strongest cognitive payoff came from Garrido et al. 2025, which applied the **violation-of-expectation paradigm from infant cognition (Spelke core knowledge)**. Latent prediction error served as "surprise". V-JEPA distinguished physically plausible from impossible videos (object permanence, shape constancy), while pixel-space predictors and MLLMs (Qwen2-VL-7B, Gemini 1.5 Pro) were near chance. A 115M model was already above chance — [arXiv 2502.11831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11831); secondary summary of the VideoMAEv2/Qwen/Gemini numbers at [bdtechtalks](https://bdtechtalks.com/2025/04/28/v-jepa-intuitive-physics/).
- **Psychophysics cue-conflict → shape vs texture bias.** Geirhos et al. (ICLR 2019, pre-window) and "Partial success in closing the gap between human and machine vision" (NeurIPS 2021) set the template of controlled stimuli plus human baselines plus error consistency — [arXiv 1811.12231](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12231), [arXiv 2106.07411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07411) (background; the NeurIPS 2021 oral status is my memory and not verified).
- **Peripheral vision (texture tiling model) → COCO-Periph (ICLR 2024).** The authors adapted Rosenholtz's TTM for DNNs and ran a psychophysics experiment comparing humans and detectors. Detectors underperform humans under simulated periphery, and training on COCO-Periph narrows the gap only partly. Networks remain less sensitive to clutter than humans — [ICLR 2024](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/18811), [paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/04ea7279a16869a980408a6977600e5d-Paper-Conference.pdf). I found **no VLM follow-up** — same search.
- **Developmental learning → child-view self-supervision (SAYCam).** Vong, Wang, Orhan & Lake, *Science* 2024, learned word–referent mappings from one child's head-cam. Orhan & Lake, *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2024, learned visual representations from a child's perspective — [Science doi 10.1126/science.adi1374](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi1374), [arXiv 2305.15372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15372) (background).
- **Kahneman dual-process → dual-system VLAs.** Fast-in-Slow (NeurIPS 2025) embeds the System 1 action module inside the System 2 VLM, reaching ~117.7 Hz with +8% in simulation and +11% in the real world, and cites Kahneman. Hume uses a System-2 value head with a System-1 denoiser. GR00T N1 pairs a diffusion System 1 with an LLM System 2 — [FiS-VLA NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/119931), [Hume arXiv 2505.21432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21432v2), [VLA survey 2505.04769](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04769). This is architecture-level metaphor. No dual-process *behavioural* prediction, such as default-interventionist conflict effects, has been tested.
- **Efference copy / forward models → action-conditioned world models.** Navigation World Models received a **CVPR 2025 Best Paper Honorable Mention** — [CVF awards](https://www.thecvf.com/?page_id=413), [CVPR 2025 awards press](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2025/News/Awards_Press). PEVA (NeurIPS 2025) predicts egocentric video from whole-body 3D pose actions — [arXiv 2506.21552](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21552v1). Neither frames or tests itself as an efference copy (same search).
- **Foveation / saccades → "thinking with images" agents.** These agents crop and zoom. The 2026 critique: only 10–12% of visual calls on a cold-start checkpoint were both *needed and used*, and released agents make unnecessary calls 36–87% of the time. The CounterCredit reward fixes this post hoc (V\* 89.5%, HR-Bench-4K 80.2%) — [arXiv 2609.22910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22910) (19 Sep 2026).
- **Event segmentation theory (Zacks) → surprise-based video memory.** Cambrian-S (Nov 2025) closes a segment when a high-surprise frame arrives — [arXiv 2511.04670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04670). Event-VStream (Jan 2026) reports that motion spikes precede semantic-similarity drops by about 2 s — [arXiv 2601.15655](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15655).

**Award landscape, 2024–2026 (CV main tracks):**
- CVPR 2025: Best Paper VGGT. Honorable mentions include MegaSaM and Navigation World Models. Best Student Paper went to Neural Inverse Rendering from Propagating Light — [CVF awards](https://www.thecvf.com/?page_id=413), [basic.ai summary](https://www.basic.ai/blog-post/cvpr-2025-top-papers-award-winners-and-notable-research).

- CVPR 2026: Best Paper "Efficiently Reconstructing Dynamic Scenes One D4RT at a Time". Best Student Paper "Native and Compact Structured Latents for 3D Generation". HMs: NitroGen, SAM 3D, ChordEdit — [CVPR 2026 closing news](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/News/Closing).
- Cognitively framed CV papers *accepted* at CVPR 2026: "Time Blindness" reports humans at 98% on temporal-only stimuli versus 0% for all Video-VLMs, 2B to 78B, with or without CoT — [project page](https://timeblindness.github.io/), [arXiv 2505.24867](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24867v1). Award status: none found.

### Inferences
- Award-level CV papers in 2024–2026 are almost all 3D/4D reconstruction or generation. A cognitive-lens paper should not expect a best paper. The realistic ceiling is **oral/highlight**, and that needs (a) a crisp named phenomenon, (b) a quantitative human-comparable law, and (c) a cheap fix or a predictive use. Garrido et al. and Time Blindness are the closest templates. Both are "a classic paradigm + a striking dissociation between model families".
- The highest-impact transfers (diffusion, JEPA, Hopfield) were **objectives or equivalences**, not benchmarks. A 34-day, 1-GPU project cannot do that. The feasible version is the "paradigm transfer with a measurable law" route.

### Gaps
- I could not verify ICLR 2026 or NeurIPS 2025 outstanding-paper lists for cognitively framed vision work. These lists were not searched in this session.
- Oral status for Garrido et al. 2025, Fast-in-Slow, and COCO-Periph is not verified.

## Q2. Which psychophysics paradigms have been turned into VLM/video-model tests, which have not, and which lenses are ripe for CVPR 2027?

### Takeaway
**Saturated**, meaning many benchmarks with human baselines already exist: mental-rotation accuracy, visual illusions, perceptual constancy as accuracy, amodal-completion reports, visual search, colour discrimination, Gestalt grouping, intuitive-physics VoE. **Not yet operationalized** (no paper found): (1) **crowding / Bouma's law** for VLMs; (2) **type-2 metacognitive sensitivity (meta-d′)** as the gate for "look again"; (3) **flow parsing / efference-copy tests** of action-conditioned world models; (4) **chronometric** mental rotation, i.e. whether reasoning-token count grows linearly with angle; (5) **gradual-change blindness** in video LLMs; (6) **Thouless/Brunswik constancy ratios** with layer localization; (7) reliability-weighted **cue combination inside vision-only foundation models** such as depth cues. The open space is no longer "is the model human-like" accuracy. It is **psychometric-function shape and chronometry**: thresholds, ratios, slopes and RT analogues. Accuracy benchmarks are crowded.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Best ratio of novelty to cost:** L1 (crowding) and L2 (meta-d′) are inference-only, ≤3 GPU-days, with no direct prior work found. Each yields a *named law*, either "Bouma's law in tokens" or "VLMs know what they don't know, not what they didn't see". L3 (flow parsing) is the most "perspective-changing" for world models, but it has infrastructure risk. L4 is the cheapest teaser (one plot of tokens vs. angle).
- L1 combines naturally with the earlier sampling/aliasing idea into a perception-law paper: aliasing (phase), crowding (spacing) and size (tokens per element). That merge strengthens both, but it raises scoop exposure in the crowded agentic-zoom cluster.
- Under the CVPR framing, each lens should be pitched as a **diagnostic that predicts real-benchmark failures plus a training-free fix**, not as "cognitive alignment". Example: crowding thresholds predict ScreenSpot-Pro errors.

## Q3. Pitfalls: what makes reviewers punish "brain-inspired" papers, and what makes a transfer rigorous?

### Takeaway
Reviewers reward **controlled manipulation of independent variables with fitted psychometric functions and a human (or normative) reference**. They punish analogy-by-naming ("hippocampal module", "System 2"), accuracy-only "human-likeness" scores, and benchmarks with no mechanism or fix. Bowers et al. make the canonical critique: DNNs "account for almost no results from psychological research", and prediction-based benchmarks do not test hypotheses about the features driving behaviour.

### Cited Findings
- Bowers et al. (BBS 2022/23, vol. 46, e385) argue that behavioural and brain-prediction benchmarks don't test which features drive predictions. They call for models that explain experiments manipulating independent variables — [Bristol](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/deep-problems-with-neural-network-models-of-human-vision/), [AAM pdf](https://research.aston.ac.uk/files/78640388/Bowersetal_2022_AAM.pdf).
- Example of the "accuracy up, human-likeness down" dissociation that reviewers find convincing: the title and premise of "More Accurate, Less Human: Gestalt Grouping in Vision Models" — [arXiv 2608.10195](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10195) (content not verified).
- Normative baselines beat raw human-similarity. BayesBench adds a **Bayesian Consistency Score** that detects Bayes-consistent shifts even when accuracy saturates — [arXiv 2512.02719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02719). The Weber-law LLM study reports fitted Weber fractions with BCa CIs — [arXiv 2603.20642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20642).
- Human baselines must come from the same stimuli. COCO-Periph ran its own psychophysics with humans — [ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/04ea7279a16869a980408a6977600e5d-Paper-Conference.pdf). Studies that borrow human norms from different tests are not comparable, and mental-rotation human scores differ across papers because tests and populations differ — [11Plus-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.20068), [J. Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence14090219).
- Reversed signatures are informative. Visual-search slopes in VLMs reverse the human present/absent asymmetry — [arXiv 2606.25066](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25066v1). "Illusion-Illusion" shows that illusion "success" can be a language prior, because models report illusions on non-illusory controls — [arXiv 2412.18613](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18613). This means every paradigm needs **blind/control stimuli**.
- Garrido et al. show the winning recipe: one paradigm (VoE), one model-internal quantity (latent surprise), and a sharp dissociation across training objectives (latent vs. pixel vs. MLLM) with ablations — [arXiv 2502.11831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11831).

### Inferences
Rigor checklist for a CVPR 2027 submission:
1. **Parametric stimulus control.** Vary one independent variable (spacing, angle, illuminant, Δ per frame) over ≥7 levels and fit a psychometric function (threshold, slope, lapse) with bootstrapped CIs. Do not report a single accuracy.
2. **Normative or human anchor.** Use either an ideal-observer prediction (MLE weights, Bouma 0.5, a linear RT–angle law) or a small, same-stimulus human study. Note that even 20 participants on Prolific need ethics approval, which is a timeline risk for Nov 16. A normative anchor avoids it.
3. **Signature, not score.** Pre-register the qualitative signature (e.g. substitution errors, similarity effect, linear slope) that distinguishes the cognitive mechanism from trivial alternatives (resolution, language prior, tokenization).
4. **Controls.** Use a blind/no-image control, a non-illusory or no-change control, a matched-token control, and checks across ≥4 model families and ≥2 scales.
5. **Mechanistic localization + fix.** Probe the encoder vs. the LLM (where is the information lost?), then apply a training-free intervention guided by the law. That turns a "cog-sci benchmark" into a CV contribution.
6. **Language.** Name the CV problem first ("high-resolution document perception fails under clutter") and the cognitive concept second, as the explanatory model. Avoid "brain-inspired" in the title.
