# CVPR Review/Selection Process and Highlight/Oral Criteria (CVPR 2024–2027 planning)

Research date: 2026-10-13. Anything for 2027 that the official pages do not state is marked **[ESTIMATED]**.

## Q1. CVPR 2027 deadline, venue, and policy changes vs CVPR 2026

### Takeaway
CVPR 2027 is June 20–25, 2027 in Seattle. Paper registration is due Nov 10, 2026 and the full paper Nov 16, 2026 (AOE, "no extension"). Decisions come Feb 25, 2027. The 2027 CFP keeps mandatory reviewing by all authors. Its LLM policy is still "being finalized", and it adds topics including "Agentic AI" and "Physical AI". Two policies were new in CVPR 2026: a Findings track/workshop for rejected-but-sound papers, and experimental compute reporting.

### Cited Findings
- **CVPR 2027 key dates (all AOE):**
  - Paper registration: Nov 10, 2026, 11:59 PM.
  - Submission: Nov 16, 2026 ("Date is fixed, no extension will be given").
  - Supplementary: Nov 23, 2026.
  - Reviews released: Jan 25, 2027; rebuttal Jan 25 – Feb 1, 2027.
  - Final decisions: Feb 25, 2027.
  - Source: [CVPR 2027 CFP](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers); same dates on [mldeadlines](https://mldeadlines.com/conference/cvpr-2027/).
- OpenCurious lists Nov 13, 2026, but it says that date was projected from the 2026 cycle before the CFP was out. Treat it as outdated — [OpenCurious](https://www.opencurious.com/ai-conference-deadlines/cvpr-2027).
- **Conference:** June 20–25, 2027, Seattle, WA. The homepage map link oddly points elsewhere, so confirm the exact venue. The CFP was posted Sep 3 and the Call for Workshops Oct 2 — [CVPR 2027 home](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027).
- **2027 dual submission window:** no substantially similar paper may be submitted elsewhere from Nov 16, 2026 to Feb 22, 2027 — [CVPR 2027 CFP](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers).
- **2027 reviewer obligation:** "All authors commit to serve as reviewers, area chairs, or senior area chairs when invited by the program chairs." Exemptions apply only in "truly exceptional circumstances", disclosed in advance and approved — [CVPR 2027 CFP](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers).
- **2027 LLM policy:** "The exact scope, permitted uses, and associated guidelines are currently being finalized." The CFP will be updated before the deadline, and there is a dedicated LLM Usage section — [CVPR 2027 CFP](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers).
- **2027 arXiv and contemporaneous work:**
  - "We do not distinguish arxiv papers and other published (conference & journal) papers."
  - Work that appeared online after **Sep 15, 2026** is generally "contemporaneous". A paper will not be rejected only for not comparing to it, but authors must still cite and discuss it.
  - Source: [CVPR 2027 CFP](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers).
- **2027 new topics:** "Agentic AI" and "Physical AI: World Models, VLA, Planning, Simulation" — [CVPR 2027 CFP](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers).
- The CVPR 2027 Author Guidelines page returned 404 when checked on 2026-10-13. Page limit, per-author cap and desk-reject rules for 2027 are therefore not yet published — [cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines).
- **CVPR 2026 dates and venue (baseline):**
  - Abstract Nov 7, 2025; paper Nov 13, 2025; supplementary Nov 20, 2025.
  - Reviews Jan 22, 2026; rebuttal Jan 22–29; decisions Feb 20, 2026.
  - Up-to-date OpenReview profiles were required for all co-authors.
  - Sources: [CVPR 2026 CFP](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/CallForPapers). Conference June 3–7, 2026, Denver — [BAAI hub](https://hub.baai.ac.cn/view/52668).
- **CVPR 2026 author policies (baseline for 2027):**
  - Page limit: "Papers are limited to eight pages, including figures and tables". Reference-only pages are extra.
  - Cap: "Each author is limited to a maximum of 25 paper submissions." Excess papers are desk-rejected.
  - LLM use by authors: "Authors may use any tools they find productive", but authors are fully responsible. Citations to non-existent works can lead to rejection.
  - Hidden prompt-injection text leads to desk rejection.
  - Links that expand content are prohibited.
  - Code is encouraged, not required.
  - Source: [CVPR 2026 Author Guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuidelines).
- **New in CVPR 2026:**
  - **Compute reporting** was experimental. Reports were "not visible to reviewers and will not influence acceptance decisions", and there were awards for efficiency and transparency.
  - A **Findings Track** was added for "technically sound papers with more incremental novelty". Area Chairs recommend papers and the authors are invited to submit.
  - Source: [CVPR 2026 Author Guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuidelines).
- **Findings in practice (2026):**
  - ACs recommended **1,717** rejected papers to a Findings Workshop.
  - Authors had to opt in, and workshop organizers did a second review.
  - Only rejected papers were recommended.
  - Source: [BAAI hub](https://hub.baai.ac.cn/view/52668) (Chinese-language report).
- **CVPR 2026 reviewer rules:**
  - "LLMs are NOT allowed to be used to write reviews or meta-reviews." Using them for background research or phrase clarity is allowed.
  - "Any reviewer whose review is deemed to be 'highly irresponsible' will face a desk rejection" of their own papers, at PC discretion.
  - Examples of irresponsible reviews: overly short, generic, factually wrong, or LLM-generated.
  - Source: [CVPR 2026 Reviewer Guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines).
- **CVPR 2025 enforcement precedent:**
  - Mandatory author reviewing was introduced "for the first time in CVPR history".
  - There was a 25-submission cap per author.
  - More than 200 papers were desk-rejected, including 19 for irresponsible reviewing and 18 for incomplete profiles.
  - Other grounds included generative AI used without verification, reference fabrication, anonymity breaches and over-length papers.
  - "Below expectations" reviews fell from 9% to 6%.
  - Source: [Voxel51 summary of CVPR 2025 opening remarks](https://voxel51.com/blog/opening-remarks-from-cvpr-2025).

### Inferences
- **[ESTIMATED]** For 2027, expect the 8-page limit, the 25-paper cap, mandatory reviewing, desk rejection for irresponsible reviewers and the ban on LLM-written reviews to carry over. All of these held in 2025 and 2026, but the 2027 Author Guidelines are not yet published.
- **[ESTIMATED]** The Findings track is likely to return. A paper that is sound but "incremental" now has a defined fallback, which makes the main-track bar on novelty explicit.
- The real deadline is effectively **Nov 10** (registration with full author list and OpenReview profiles). Profiles without an institutional email can take up to two weeks to moderate, so co-authors should set them up now.
- Contemporaneous cutoff is Sep 15, 2026. Anything posted on arXiv before then (for example, competing tracking papers) must be compared or discussed.

### Gaps
- The CVPR 2027 LLM policy, page limit, per-author cap and any Findings track continuation were not announced as of 2026-10-13.
- CVPR 2027 program chairs were not retrieved.

- **Terminology:**
  - CVPR's public tiers are **Poster / Highlight / Oral**, plus award candidates — [Paper Copilot](https://papercopilot.com/venue-overview/cvpr-2025-venue-overview/).

  - The 2026 preliminary decision emails said Poster/Spotlight/Oral formats would be announced later — [BAAI hub](https://hub.baai.ac.cn/view/52668).

### Inferences
- 2026 roughly doubled the award-candidate pool (15 to 74) and raised the oral count (90/≈95 to 141). The orals' share of accepted papers stayed near 3.3–3.4%, so tiers scale with volume.
- **[ESTIMATED] 2027:** if submissions grow 15–25%, that is about 18.5k–20k papers. At a 22–25% acceptance rate, about 4.1k–5k would be accepted, with about 550–700 highlights and 135–170 orals. This is extrapolation, not an announcement.
- A target of "highlight or better" means landing in about the top 3–4% of submissions, which is about 15% of accepted papers.

## Q3. How highlights and orals are chosen

### Takeaway
Tiers are set after acceptance. AC triplets nominate papers, and the Program Chairs make the final assignment. Reviewer scores give the initial ranking and ACs push papers up or down. There is no published score threshold.

### Cited Findings
- "Area Chair triplets" nominate papers for spotlights, orals and awards. Program Chairs make the final call on "assigning papers to the oral/spotlight/poster categories" — [CVPR 2026 Reviewer Guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines).
- Freeman (CVPR 2013 PC) on the paper selection meeting:
  - "The reviewers' scores give an initial ranking; the area chairs then push papers up or down."
  - "CVPR, ICCV: intermediate level of discussion" (compared with NeurIPS score-driven and SIGGRAPH discussion-heavy).
  - ACs "decide whether to reject or accept as poster or oral talk."
  - Source: [Freeman, "How to write a good CVPR submission" (2014 slides)](https://www.cs.torontomu.ca/~wangcs/resources\How-to-write-a-good-CVPR-submission.pdf).
- Decisions are "preliminary", conditional on plagiarism, duplicate-submission and reviewer/AC-conduct checks. Tier formats were announced later than accept/reject — [BAAI hub](https://hub.baai.ac.cn/view/52668).
- Oral/Highlight assignments are published roughly two weeks before the conference — [search summary of a CVPR 2026 source; not independently verified](https://www.paperdigest.org/2026/04/cvpr-2026-papers-highlights/).

### Inferences
- What gets a highlight is AC enthusiasm, not mean score alone: a meta-review that clearly states the contribution, high reviewer agreement, and broad interest. Clear "insight" claims that an AC can repeat in a nomination likely help.

### Gaps
- No public rubric or score cutoff for highlight/oral selection was found.

## Q4. What the official reviewer guidelines say about novelty, simple methods and SOTA

### Takeaway
The official text explicitly protects papers that do not beat SOTA and requires "not novel" claims to cite specific prior work. It says nothing explicit about simple methods or analysis papers. The 2026 Findings track, however, signals that "incremental novelty" is grounds for main-track rejection.

### Cited Findings
- A paper that "does not exceed the state-of-the-art accuracy on an existing benchmark dataset is not grounds for rejection by itself". Reviewers weigh novelty and potential impact. Reviewers are told to welcome bold ideas even if tested on few datasets — [CVPR 2026 Reviewer Guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines).
- "Claims in a review that the submitted work 'has been done before' MUST be backed up with specific references." Strengths should summarize the most interesting novel aspects — [CVPR 2026 Reviewer Guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines).
- "Bullet lists with one short phrase per bullet are NOT a detailed review" — [CVPR 2026 Reviewer Guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines).
- The Findings track targets "technically sound papers with more incremental novelty" — [CVPR 2026 Author Guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuidelines).

### Inferences
- In practice, soundness alone now routes a paper toward Findings. The main track, and especially highlights, needs a clearly articulated new idea or insight. A missing SOTA result is defensible on paper, but only if the novelty and impact case is made explicitly.

### Gaps
- No official CVPR text was found on how to value pure analysis or "insight" papers.

## Q5. Advice from senior researchers on high-impact, simple-but-insightful papers

### Takeaway
Michael Black argues that simplicity *is* novelty and that hindsight bias is the main reviewer error. Freeman, quoting Kajiya and Adelson, stresses that the contribution must be stated up front, because overloaded reviewers will not dig for it.

### Cited Findings
- **Michael Black, "Novelty in Science" (Jan 2022):**
  - Reviewers mistake complexity, difficulty and technicality for novelty. "We might be better served by removing the word 'novelty' from the review instructions" in favour of "beauty".
  - "I value simplicity over unnecessary complexity; the simpler the better."
  - "The novelty, however, must be evaluated before the idea existed."
  - A new dataset, a new use of an old method, or a simpler algorithm can each be novel.
  - A simple idea that beats SOTA is "most likely not trivial".
  - Source: [Perceiving Systems blog](https://perceiving-systems.blog/en/news/novelty-in-science); also on [Medium](https://medium.com/@black_51980/novelty-in-science-8f1fd1a0a143).
- Black on a small change with a large effect: "If nobody thought to change that one term, then it is ipso facto novel" — quoted in [Seidenschwarz et al., arXiv 2206.04656](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.04656). That paper is itself a "simple cues" tracking paper that used this argument.
- **Bill Freeman (MIT, CVPR 2013 PC), 2014 talk:**
  - A slide plots career impact against quality: only "creative, original and good" papers have lots of impact.
  - He recommends a "dynamite introduction".
  - "Underutilized technique: explain the main idea with a simple, toy example."
  - Source: [Freeman slides](https://www.cs.torontomu.ca/~wangcs/resources\How-to-write-a-good-CVPR-submission.pdf).
- **Kajiya, quoted by Freeman:**
  - "The most dangerous mistake you can make when writing your paper is assuming that the reviewer will understand the point of your paper."
  - State "what is really new in your paper (and what isn't), why it's so neat", and state "the implications of your solution".
  - "If the reviewers misunderstand your paper... you're dead."
  - Source: [Freeman slides](https://www.cs.torontomu.ca/~wangcs/resources\How-to-write-a-good-CVPR-submission.pdf).
- **Ted Adelson's formula, quoted by Freeman:**
  1. State the problem and why the audience should care.
  2. Explain why existing solutions are unsatisfactory.
  3. Present your solution, compare it, and say why it is better.
  4. Cover related techniques used on other problems.
  - Source: [Freeman slides](https://www.cs.torontomu.ca/~wangcs/resources\How-to-write-a-good-CVPR-submission.pdf).
- **Jia-Bin Huang, "Deep Paper Gestalt":**
  - A tongue-in-cheek classifier trained on CVPR/ICCV papers judged papers by visual appearance alone and could "reject 50% of the bad papers while wrongly reject only 0.4% of the good papers".
  - Its suggestions: a teaser figure up front, colorful figures, a full last page.
  - This shows how much first-glance presentation shapes reviewer priors.
  - Source: [arXiv 1812.08775](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08775).

### Inferences
- For a "simple method plus insight" paper: lead with the one-sentence insight and a teaser figure, give a toy example that makes the mechanism obvious, pre-empt the "obvious in hindsight" critique, and frame the novelty type explicitly (new problem formulation, new evaluation, or new insight).

### Gaps
- No retrievable primary sources were found for Devi Parikh's or Kaiming He's specific advice in this pass. Do not attribute claims to them without a source.
- Jia-Bin Huang's substantive writing and research advice (his talks and X threads) was not retrieved.

## Q6. Subject areas with the highest submission volume (saturation)

### Takeaway
For CVPR 2026, the highest-volume areas named by the program chairs were:
1. Image/video synthesis and generation
2. Vision, language and reasoning
3. Multi-modal learning
4. 3D from multiview and sensors
5. Medical/biological vision and cell microscopy, which "grew substantially"

### Cited Findings
- 2026 areas with the most submissions: image and video synthesis and generation; vision, language and reasoning; multi-modal learning; 3-D from multiview and sensors; medical and biological vision and cell microscopy — [Robotics & Automation News (CVPR press)](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/05/27/cvpr-2026-fields-16000-paper-submissions-on-technical-advances-in-ai/101941/).
- PC co-chair Chen Change Loy: "the emphasis in medical and biological vision and cell microscopy grew substantially this year" — [same source](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/05/27/cvpr-2026-fields-16000-paper-submissions-on-technical-advances-in-ai/101941/).
- The CVPR 2027 CFP adds "Agentic AI" and "Physical AI: World Models, VLA, Planning, Simulation" as topics — [CVPR 2027 CFP](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers).

### Inferences
- Cell microscopy is explicitly named as a growing area for CVPR 2026. That is relevant to a cell-tracking submission: there is more AC/reviewer capacity in the area, but also more competition. A clear insight framing matters more than benchmark gains.
- Generative, VLM and 3D areas are the most saturated, where incremental SOTA papers face the most competition.

### Gaps
- No numeric per-area submission counts for 2025 or 2026 were found. The Paper Copilot 2025 page showed "No category data". CVPR 2025 opening-remarks area stats were not retrieved.
