# Complementary lenses for an MLLM "readout/arbitration bottleneck" paper: (A) crowding, (B) type-2 metacognition, (C) RL changes readout and not perception

Scope note: today is 2026-10-13. The CVPR 2027 deadline is 2026-11-16, and the compute budget is one H200. Work posted after 2026-09-15 counts as contemporaneous. The anchor claim that each angle has to support or challenge is this: MLLM failures arise at a late-layer readout or arbitration stage, not in perception. The closest anchor paper is [Arbitration Failure, Not Perceptual Blindness (arXiv 2604.09364)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09364). All compute figures are **my own estimates** and have not been measured. "Background, not re-fetched" marks well-known facts that I cite from prior knowledge and did not re-open in this session.

## Q1 (Angle A). Has anyone measured psychometric crowding functions (Bouma-style critical spacing) in VLMs, and can crowding be split into encoder vs readout?

### Takeaway
I found **no paper that measures a crowding psychometric function, a critical spacing or Bouma scaling in a VLM**, in pixel units or in token units. I also found none that localizes crowding to the encoder or to the readout. The closest prior work has three parts. First, a qualitative note in "VLMs are blind" that models reach near 100% once shapes and letters are spaced further apart. Second, a Flanker-congruency benchmark that does not report a spacing sweep. Third, CNN crowding studies from 2019–2021 that predate VLMs. Novelty is high and scoop risk is low to moderate. The main risk is a reviewer reading the work as "just a benchmark", so it must come with an encoder-vs-readout localization and a link to real benchmark errors.

### Cited Findings
**Closest VLM prior work**
- "Vision language models are blind" (BlindTest, arXiv 2407.06581, v6 Mar 2025) has seven tasks, including "which letter is circled in a word" and overlapping circles. Four SOTA VLMs average 58.07%, and the best model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) reaches 77.84%. The authors report that VLMs "perform at near-100% accuracy when much more space is added to separate shapes and letters". That is a qualitative spacing effect, with no psychometric function, threshold or units analysis — [arXiv 2407.06581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06581)
- "Increasing Computation Resolves Conflicts in VLMs" (arXiv 2505.18969, v2 Feb 2026) has 4,410 tasks across 7 conflict paradigms, including Stroop and **Flanker**, run on 47 VLMs. Models show robust congruency effects. Larger models resolve conflict better but fall below chance on incongruent high-conflict trials. The abstract does not say whether target–flanker spacing was varied. This is the Eriksen *attentional* flanker task, not crowding — [arXiv 2505.18969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18969)
- Neither the targeted searches ("crowding" + multimodal LLM 2026, "Bouma" + transformer, "critical spacing" + visual tokens, COCO-Periph + VLM) nor the earlier lens survey turned up a VLM crowding study. The only "crowding" LLM hit was "cognitive crowding" in text LLMs (arXiv 2604.17174), which is unrelated — [arXiv 2604.17174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17174); earlier survey: `../Góc nhìn đột phá CVPR 2027/cognitive_neuro_lenses.md` (row L1)
- COCO-Periph (ICLR 2024) adapts the Texture Tiling Model to 5°/10°/15°/20° eccentricity for detectors such as Faster-RCNN. Detectors lose accuracy with eccentricity much faster than humans do, and training on COCO-Periph shifts critical eccentricity out by about 5°. I found no VLM follow-up — [ICLR 2024 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/04ea7279a16869a980408a6977600e5d-Paper-Conference.pdf), [summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/coco-periph-bridging-the-gap-between-human-and-machine-perception-in-the/wrk_9ab5e65b75185a2050c575ad001c1d49)

**Crowding in pre-VLM DNNs (shows that "DNN crowding ≠ human crowding")**
- In DenseNet-121, paired flankers crowd more than single flankers at all distances, and accuracy never returns to the unflanked level even at the largest spacing. Humans are uncrowded beyond about 0.5× eccentricity. The authors conclude that "crowding in humans is unlike that in convolutional neural networks" — [arXiv 1903.00258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.00258)
- In AlexNet-layer readouts of vernier offsets, crowded stimuli were *better* detected than uncrowded ones across layers, which is the opposite of humans — [PLOS Comp Bio "Beyond Bouma's window"](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1006580)

**Human anchors (normative predictions to test against)**
- Bouma's law puts critical spacing at about 0.5× eccentricity, and flanker similarity raises crowding — [PMC9525686](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9525686/), [JOV target-flanker similarity](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2810932)
- Critical spacing corresponds to a roughly constant cortical distance. Across about 50 observers, crowding distance tracks **V4 surface area (~1.4 mm on V4 map)**, not V1–V3. This is "spacing is constant in the native units of the pooling map", the direct analogue of "constant in token units" — [Nature Comms 2025 "Human V4 size predicts crowding distance"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59101-w)
- Crowding as optimal integration with context. In humans, substitution (reporting the flanker) is a signature of crowding — [PMC9525686](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9525686/), [Mixture-modeling of crowding errors, Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-10685-z)

**Supporting evidence for an encoder-vs-readout split**
- The vision-tower probe separates perturbed from clean images at 0.72–0.79 accuracy on "insensitive" samples, yet the argmax token changes on only 2–11%, so the encoder–LLM gap is above 0.65 on all six VLMs (posted 1 Sep 2026) — [arXiv 2609.00868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00868)
- In slant-from-texture, linear probes on the Qwen2.5-VL-3B vision encoder strongly encode geometry, so the error is attributed to language readout — [arXiv 2606.06714](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06714)

### Inferences
- **Novelty margin (my assessment):** this would be the first parametric crowding function for VLMs, giving (i) critical spacing s_c, (ii) its units (pixels vs ViT patches vs merged tokens), (iii) an error-type decomposition into substitution vs random vs omission, and (iv) a layer at which target identity is lost. BlindTest supplies a one-sentence qualitative precedent, so the claim must be "first *quantitative law and localization*", not "first to notice spacing matters".
- **Falsifiable predictions:**
  - P-A1 (units): with letters or digits rendered at fixed pixel size and `max_pixels` varied, the image is rescaled so that pixel spacing per token changes. If s_c stays constant **in merged-token units** and moves in pixels, crowding is set by tokenization. Qwen2.5-VL uses 14-px patches with 2×2 merging, about 28 px per LLM token (background, not re-fetched: [Qwen2.5-VL report arXiv 2502.13923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13923)). Falsifier: s_c constant in pixels.
  - P-A2 (substitution at readout): wrong answers are dominated by **flanker identities**, as in humans, more than chance among the 25 other letters. Linear probes on vision-encoder patch features at the target location decode target identity well above the model's verbal accuracy at spacings below s_c.
  - Together, P-A2 gives the integration claim: **"pooling/crowding starts in the encoder/merger, but the substitution choice is made at readout."** The opposite outcome, where probes also fail below s_c, is also publishable. It would give a clean case where the bottleneck *is* perceptual, a boundary condition that strengthens the arbitration paper by showing the authors can tell the two apart.
  - P-A3 (external validity): per-model s_c in tokens predicts ScreenSpot-Pro or DocVQA small-text error rates, for example via inter-element spacing in tokens of the ground-truth target vs its nearest neighbour. Falsifier: no rank correlation across models and resolution settings.
  - P-A4 (similarity): same-category flankers (letters among letters) raise s_c compared with dissimilar flankers (letters among shapes), matching human target–flanker similarity effects ([JOV](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2810932)).
- **Minimal experiment:** synthetic canvases with a target letter and two or four flankers. Sweep spacing in about 12 steps, measured in tokens, and use 3 `max_pixels` settings, 3 flanker types and 3 positions, about 30k single-token trials per model. Run 3–4 models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B, LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B). Fit Weibull psychometric curves per condition and run a multinomial error analysis. Use patch-level linear probes on encoder and merger outputs, plus a per-LLM-layer logit lens on the answer position. Optional: the encoder probe with the "full-sequence image-token patching" of 2604.09364 to see whether a clean-target patch at the arbitration layer flips a crowded trial. **Compute (my estimate): under 1 H200-day in total.** That covers about 0.2 day for vLLM behaviour (single-token answers), about 0.3 day for feature extraction plus probes, and about 0.3 day for patching. Correlating with ScreenSpot-Pro/DocVQA needs only existing eval runs, about 0.2 day.
- **Scoop risk (my assessment): low to moderate.** The idea is cheap and obvious to cognitive-science groups, and VSS/CCN 2026 abstracts were not checked. The arXiv sweep for Aug–Oct 2026 turned up nothing.
- **Role in the paper:** this is best as **one chapter** ("a controllable psychophysical probe of the encoder→readout split"), not as the headline. As a standalone headline it reads like "Bouma's law in tokens", which is a strong named result but a narrower story.

### Gaps
- The full BlindTest paper was not read to check whether its spacing ablation is parametric. Only the abstract claim is verified.
- I did not verify whether 2505.18969 varies flanker spacing in its body text (the abstract is silent).
- I did not check VSS 2026, CCN 2026 or the JOV special issues for VLM crowding abstracts.
- No source gives a per-token pixel geometry for InternVL3.5 or LLaVA-OV-1.5 in this session. These must be read from the configs.

## Q2 (Angle B). Has anyone measured meta-d′ or type-2 AUROC for VLMs, split by perception-limited vs knowledge-limited failures, and can a training-free gate decide when to re-look?

### Takeaway
I found **no paper reporting meta-d′, or type-2 AUROC framed as metacognition, for VLMs split by "failed to see" vs "didn't know"**. Type-2 AUROC and meta-d′ are now standard for *text* LLMs: MIRROR, the medical metacognition study (Aug 2026), and the Metacognition-in-LLMs survey (Jul 2026). The nearest VLM work comes in three groups. (i) "Failing to See or Failing to Know?" (Jul 2026) attributes knowledge-VQA errors to pre- vs post-recognition from hidden states, but without type-2 metrics. (ii) A degradation study (Jul 2026) shows that verbalized confidence is at chance while token probability tracks errors, **except that under severe underexposure both collapse to chance**. That is direct, though small-scale, evidence for "doesn't know it didn't see". (iii) Training-free uncertainty dissections and zoom heuristics (UG-Search, ZoomEye, Zoom Consistency). CounterCredit (Sep 2026) is a *trained* RL credit-assignment method, not a pre-call metacognitive gate. Novelty is moderate to high, and scoop risk is moderate because the uncertainty/zoom area is crowded.

### Cited Findings
**Closest VLM work**
- "Failing to See or Failing to Know?" (Vo, Vazhentsev, Shelmanov, Baldwin, Kementchedjhieva; v1 6 Jul 2026, v4 4 Aug 2026) builds a tree-structured attribution of knowledge-VQA failures across 2 datasets and 4 VLMs. Failures occur both before and after entity recognition. "Visual token representations are most informative for recognition-related decisions", and prompt hidden states predict answer success better than visual signals. Factual-access attribution remains weak. Pre-generation signals support routing to fixes such as image repair or factual evidence. No meta-d′ or type-2 framing appears in the abstract — [arXiv 2607.04683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04683)
- "Small VLMs Know When They Are Wrong But Cannot Say So" (Ferdous, 24 Jul 2026) tests Qwen2-VL-2B and SmolVLM on 6 degradations × 3 severities, 3,800 predictions. Qwen2-VL verbalized confidence sits at 0.87–0.90 with error-detection AUROC 0.39–0.75 (around 0.5 typical), while internal token probability gives AUROC 0.92–0.99. **Under severe underexposure, accuracy falls from 0.99 to 0.22 (Qwen2-VL) and from 0.97 to 0.42 (SmolVLM), yet both confidence signals barely change and internal error detection drops to chance** — [arXiv 2607.22034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22034)
- "Dissecting Training-Free Uncertainty Estimation in MLLMs" (Seifi, Dorovatas, Li, Gal, Aljundi; 1 Sep 2026) groups methods as token-level, verbalized or semantic. No family dominates: token entropy at T=1 is best for short answers, verbalized abstention for sentence-length answers, and semantic methods for long-form. The abstract mentions no perception/knowledge split and no type-2 metric — [arXiv 2609.22206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22206)
- In the Visual Insensitivity Gap paper, VSI (sensitivity of next-token output to blurring the relevant region) reaches AUROC 0.85–0.87 for MC reasoning on capable VLMs. It is weaker than softmax confidence for factuality, and the authors recommend it only as a conditional ensemble component (1 Sep 2026) — [arXiv 2609.00868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00868)
- VL-Calibration splits confidence into visual and reasoning parts, and Zoom Consistency uses agreement across zoom steps as a free confidence signal on ScreenSpot-Pro — [arXiv 2604.09529](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.09529), [arXiv 2604.15376](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15376)
- UG-Search is a training-free method that picks the crop with the lowest token-probability uncertainty. Search snippets report Qwen2.5-VL-7B going from 76.4 to 85.3 on V* and from 71.0 to 74.3 on HR-4K (**snippet values, not verified in the paper**). ZoomEye uses Yes/No logits to decide where to look and when to stop — [arXiv 2510.00705 (Pith summary)](https://pith.science/paper/2510.00705), [ZoomEye arXiv 2411.16044](https://pith.science/paper/2411.16044)
- Older calibration work finds that VLMs overestimate certainty, that confidence lags accuracy under corruption, and that VLMs lack epistemic/aleatoric self-awareness — [Overconfidence is Key, TrustNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.trustnlp-1.13.pdf), [arXiv 2504.03440](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03440), [CertainlyUncertain arXiv 2407.01942](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01942)

**CounterCredit (the named comparator)**
- "When Should a VLM Look? Paying Only for Visual Calls That Were Needed and Used" (Peng et al., 19 Sep 2026, so **contemporaneous**). On a cold-start checkpoint, only 10–12% of visual calls were both needed and used. Released agents make spurious calls 36–87% of the time, and about two-thirds of outcome-reward credit goes to calls that were neither. Method: a decision value (visual branch vs answering now) plus an evidence value (returned crop vs random same-size patches), "cashback/rent" credit, and a dual-channel GRPO advantage. Results: +6.3 to +9.4 points over outcome-only GRPO; V* 89.5, HR-4K 80.2, HR-8K 76.4; 1.78 calls/question vs 1.84; spurious-call rate 31–36%; Qwen3-VL-8B base improves from 75.4 to 80.8 average — [arXiv 2609.22910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22910)

**Type-2 / meta-d′ in models (text, or vision non-VLM)**
- Medical-reasoning LLMs report AUROC2 as the primary metacognitive sensitivity metric and meta-d′/d′ as exploratory. One cited value is AUROC2 = 0.876 on forced-choice trials (Aug 2026) — [arXiv 2608.14552](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14552)
- MIRROR (hierarchical metacognitive calibration benchmark for LLMs) and the survey "Metacognition in LLMs" (Jul 2026) are text-only — [arXiv 2604.19809](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19809), [arXiv 2607.11881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11881)
- A test-time model-selection paper uses sliding-window meta-d′ for image *classifiers* (not VLMs) — [arXiv 2512.10451](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10451)
- "LLMs Know When They Know, but Do Not Act on It" proposes a metacognitive harness for test-time scaling in text LLMs. It is the closest analogue to "know but don't act", that is, readout — [arXiv 2605.14186](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14186v1)
- Methods: meta-d′ is the type-1 d′ that an ideal metacognitive observer would need to produce the observed confidence; meta-d′/d′ is efficiency (HMeta-d) — [Fleming 2017, Neurosci. Conscious.](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2017/1/nix007/3748261)
- Human anchor: in peripheral vision, visual confidence tracks rising internal noise with eccentricity (bioRxiv 2026). This normative contrast says humans *do* know when they failed to see — [bioRxiv 2026.01.28.702447](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.28.702447v1)

### Inferences
- **Novelty margin (my assessment):** the novel element is the **within-item perception manipulation plus type-2 metrics, with the signal read at different depths**. That means comparing meta-d′/d′ for the same question when perception is degraded (downsample, crop-out, crowd, darken) vs when knowledge is removed (counterfactual or rare-entity questions with a clean image), and measuring that confidence at the encoder, at mid-layer arbitration, and at the output. 2607.04683 attributes errors but does not measure metacognitive *efficiency*. 2607.22034 shows the collapse in one degradation with 2 small models and no SDT framing.
- **Falsifiable predictions:**
  - P-B1: meta-d′/d′ (output token probability) is **substantially lower for perception-limited errors than for knowledge-limited errors** at matched type-1 accuracy. In the paper's terms, VLMs "know what they don't know, not what they didn't see". Falsifier: equal efficiency.
  - P-B2 (readout link): a linear probe for correctness on **mid-layer image-token states** (at or before the arbitration crossover of 2604.09364) restores type-2 AUROC on perception-limited items, while output confidence does not. So the "failed-to-see" signal exists internally but is lost at readout. This mirrors 2607.22034, where internal signals beat verbalized ones, and 2609.00868, where the encoder detects the perturbation but the answer does not move.
  - P-B3 (gate): a training-free gate, "zoom/re-look if mid-layer probe confidence < τ", recovers most of the accuracy of always-zoom on V*/HR-Bench at a fraction of the calls. It beats an output-entropy gate in spurious-call rate. Compare on CounterCredit's metrics (accuracy, calls/question, spurious-call rate), noting that CounterCredit is trained and the gate is not.
- **Minimal experiment:** (1) Items: V* and HR-Bench-4K (perception-limited); a knowledge set such as InfoSeek or the 2607.04683 datasets; and **matched pairs** made by downsampling or occluding the relevant ROI in the same items. (2) Confidence readouts: token probability, verbalized confidence, P(True), and a per-layer linear correctness probe trained on a held-out split. (3) Metrics: AUROC2 and meta-d′ via an HMeta-d / metadpy-style MLE fit (needs a binned confidence scale, for example 4–6 bins). (4) Gate: crop or zoom with a fixed ROI proposer (ZoomEye-style or ground-truth ROI as an upper bound). **Compute (my estimate): 1–2 H200-days** for 3 models × about 5k items × 2–3 conditions, plus hidden-state dumps. The gate evaluation adds about 0.5 day.
- **Scoop risk (my assessment): moderate.** Calibration/uncertainty for MLLMs is active (2609.22206 on 1 Sep, 2609.00868 on 1 Sep, CounterCredit on 19 Sep). The specific *meta-d′ × perception-vs-knowledge × layer* triple is unclaimed in what I found, but a team could add it as a section with little effort.
- **Role in the paper:** this is a strong **chapter** ("the readout bottleneck is also a metacognitive bottleneck"). It is also the angle most likely to yield a **practical, training-free deliverable** (the gate), which CVPR reviewers reward. As an *alternative headline*, "VLMs know what they don't know, but not what they didn't see" is catchy but must survive the comparison with CounterCredit and the uncertainty baselines.

### Gaps
- I did not read full texts of 2607.04683 or 2609.22206, so their per-category AUROCs and whether they include degraded-image conditions are unknown.
- The UG-Search numbers come from a third-party summary and are unverified.
- I found no published estimate of how many bins or trials meta-d′ needs to be stable for LLM confidences. Plan a bootstrap.
- I did not search for "VLM type-2" work in non-arXiv venues (e.g. CogSci 2026).

## Q3 (Angle C). Has anyone done a layer-wise base-vs-RL comparison for VLMs showing that RL changes readout rather than perception, and which public checkpoint pairs exist?

### Takeaway
I found **no paper that does a layer-wise probe/patching comparison of a base VLM against its RL (GRPO/R1-style) checkpoint** that separates encoder, projector and LLM-readout changes. Behavioural and attention-level evidence leans toward "RL mostly changes how evidence is used". The evidence: the Perception-R1 McNemar null (n=50), "More Thinking, Less Seeing" (lower visual attention in deeper layers of reasoning models), asymmetric reasoning-vs-perception optimization (May/Sep 2026), and pass@k boundary results. There is a direct counter-claim: "RL makes MLLMs see better than SFT" (PIVOT) reports that RL *reshapes* the vision encoder. A methodological template already exists for **action** post-training (2608.08904, Aug 2026: per-layer probes plus MLP-write ablations on a weight-matched base/VLA pair). Many weight-matched base/RL VLM pairs are public and verified on HF. Novelty is moderate to high, and scoop risk is the **highest of the three** because the topic is hot and the template is published.

### Cited Findings
**Behavioural and attention-level evidence**
- Perception-R1 (2506.07218, v3 Mar 2026) applies a McNemar test on **50 random MathVista problems**. Accuracy-only RLVR (on Geometry3K) vs base gives discordant counts 1/5 for Qwen2-VL-7B (p=0.22) and 2/4 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (p=0.69), so perception is not significantly different. Perception-R1 itself gives 2/10, p=0.04. Perception errors make up 68–78% of incorrect CoTs (MathVista/MathVerse). Perception-R1-7B uses 1,442 samples, base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-IT, MathVista 74.2 — [arXiv 2506.07218v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07218v3). **Note: n=50 is a weak test, and a layer-wise analysis would substantially strengthen or overturn it.**
- "More Thinking, Less Seeing?" (NeurIPS 2025): multimodal reasoning models give visual tokens less attention, **with a further decrease in deeper layers**, and show sparse, dispersed visual attention. Longer reasoning drifts toward language priors (the RH-AUC metric and RH-Bench) — [arXiv 2505.21523](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21523v3), [NeurIPS poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/poster/118585)
- "On Asymmetric Optimization of Reasoning and Perception in VLM Post-Training" (Wu, Lin, Chang, Peng; v1 28 May 2026, v2 2 Sep 2026) uses two synthetic tasks to separate perception from reasoning. Post-training helps reasoning more. For RL the cause is **reward coupling**: outcome reward correlates more with reasoning. A perception-aware reward adds up to +6.0 points and a surrogate adds +2.2. The abstract mentions no layer-wise analysis — [arXiv 2605.29496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29496)
- In "Credit Where It is Due" (Feb 2026), only about 15% of tokens show strong visual–text attention coupling ("anchors"). RLVR credit concentrates on them, and training only on low-connectivity tokens degrades performance — [arXiv 2602.11455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11455)
- Answer-only RL *lowered* HallusionBench from about 43.8% to 37.7% in one CVPR 2026 line of work — [arXiv 2512.12487](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12487); see also [CVPR 2026 Perception-centric PRM](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Min_Improving_Vision-language_Models_with_Perception-centric_Process_Reward_Models_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)

**RLVR capacity debate**
- "Does RL Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?" (Yue et al., arXiv 2504.13837) was a **NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper runner-up**. RLVR wins at small k, but base models win at large pass@k across math, code and **visual reasoning (Qwen2.5-VL-7B on MathVista/MathVision)** — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/537d5aa768c2d534016a4d06f87bc8fb-Abstract-Conference.html), [project page](https://limit-of-rlvr.github.io/), [award summary](https://howaiworks.ai/blog/neurips-2025-best-paper-awards-announcement)
- A counterpoint for vision, "Does RLVR Extend Reasoning Boundaries?" (Ariadne, ACL 2026), finds that RLVR solves synthetic spatial/maze problems where the base model stays at 0% under any pass@k, and that this transfers to navigation — [arXiv 2511.00710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00710), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.2102/)
- Pass@k inversion concentrates on "boundary prompts" (2026). Activation Replay mitigates narrowing in LMMs — [arXiv 2607.20543](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20543v1), [arXiv 2511.19972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19972)

**Where RL updates land (text LLMs)**
- RL fine-tunes a sparse subnetwork: typically 70% or more of weights are unchanged, updates are nearly full-rank, sparsity is spread across matrices (not a layer subset), and LayerNorm is almost untouched — [arXiv 2505.11711](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11711). A model-merging analysis instead finds updates concentrated in intermediate layers — [arXiv 2606.18521](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18521). **These conflict on layer distribution.**
- Token-level base-vs-RL divergence is near zero at most positions — [arXiv 2603.22446](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22446v1)

**Counter-evidence: RL changes the encoder**
- "RL makes MLLMs see better than SFT" (Song, Yun, Han, Choo, Heo; Oct 2025, v2 Apr 2026): RL beats SFT on vision-heavy VQA, and RL-trained **vision encoders** give "stronger and precisely localized" features (ImageNet classification, segmentation, gradient maps). This led to PIVOT, which uses under 1% of pretraining compute. In this setting the encoder is *trained* — [arXiv 2510.16333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16333)
- A position paper reports that tuning only the vision encoder with RL can match full tuning on some tasks (Table 7) — [arXiv 2507.16863](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.16863)

**Methodological template (post-training, not RL)**
- "From Recovery to Drop-off" (Hackett et al., 9 Aug 2026, ECCV 2026 EMR workshop) uses a weight-matched pair, Molmo2-ER (base VLM) vs MolmoAct2-LIBERO (VLA). Depth is probed at every decoder layer. The VLA is worse at every layer (the "floor"), and its late-layer decodability collapses where the base model's keeps rising (the "cliff"). Ablating late-layer **MLP writes** recovers most of the cliff, while attention ablations do not — [arXiv 2608.08904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08904)
- Per-layer MLP probes find the correct count decodable at intermediate layers before late layers shift toward the language prior — [arXiv 2606.06890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06890)

### Inferences
- **Novelty margin (my assessment):** this would be the first **weight-matched, layer-resolved, causal** (probe + logit-lens + cross-model activation/weight grafting) base-vs-RL study for VLMs, over 5 or more RL recipes and 2 or more model families. It turns the n=50 McNemar null and the attention observations into a mechanism. The 2608.08904 template shows the approach is accepted but has not been applied to RL VLMs. The PIVOT counter-claim makes it a **genuine empirical question** rather than a foregone conclusion, which helps novelty.
- **Critical first check (a cheap reviewer trap):** compute per-module weight deltas (‖ΔW‖/‖W‖) for ViT, merger/projector and each LLM block for every pair. If a recipe **froze the ViT**, then "perception unchanged in the encoder" is true by construction and must not be claimed as a finding. In that case the test moves to **image-token states inside the LLM** (early vs late layers). This step is CPU-only and takes minutes.
- **Falsifiable predictions:**
  - P-C1: for RL-only recipes (VL-Rethinker, MM-Eureka, ThinkLite, Perception-R1, VLAA-Thinker, MiMo SFT→RL), linear-probe accuracy for visual attributes on image tokens is **unchanged (|Δ| < 1–2 pts) up to the arbitration crossover**. Representation drift (CKA/mutual-kNN vs base) is concentrated in **late layers** and at the answer/reasoning positions. Falsifier: early-layer probe gains, or drift concentrated in the encoder or early layers.
  - P-C2 (causal): grafting the RL model's **late blocks only** into the base model reproduces most of the RL accuracy gain. Grafting early blocks or the ViT reproduces little. Measure the fraction of gain recovered per grafted block range. The mirror experiment, base late blocks into the RL model, removes the gain.
  - P-C3 (perception reward): Perception-R1 (perception reward) vs accuracy-only RL shows that the perception reward **also acts mainly at readout**, so its probe curves match the base model's and the difference is in the late logit gap. If instead it raises mid-layer decodability, that is the interesting exception and also publishable.
  - P-C4 (link to the anchor claim): the 2604.09364 "logit gap" (final-layer margin) rises after RL on items where encoding strength is unchanged.
- **Minimal experiment:** 6–8 pairs, all 7–9B. Use a probe suite (attributes, counts, positions, OCR snippets; about 3k items) and per-layer hidden-state dumps for base and RL models (about 1 GPU-hour per model). Then linear probes, CKA/mKNN, logit lens, and layer-block grafting (about 30 hybrid models × 2k items). **Compute (my estimate): about 2–3 H200-days** in total, or about 50 GPU-hours for the analysis alone, consistent with the earlier note `../Ý tưởng nghiên cứu CVPR 2027/open_problems_vlm_representation.md`. An optional own GRPO+LoRA run with ViT frozen vs unfrozen as a controlled ablation would take about 1–3 extra days (rollout-bound) and is risky for the timeline.
- **Scoop risk (my assessment): moderate to high.** Ingredients are public, 2605.29496 (v2 Sep 2026) is one step away from a layer analysis, and 2608.08904 has a template that can be copied directly. Mitigate by making it a chapter of the arbitration paper, not a standalone claim.
- **Role in the paper:** this is the **strongest chapter**: "RL post-training moves the arbitration stage, not the perceptual code". It is also a plausible **alternative headline** if P-C1 and P-C2 hold cleanly across families, since it directly explains the NeurIPS'25 runner-up result mechanistically for VLMs. If PIVOT-style encoder changes appear in some recipes, report the result as "RL changes readout unless the encoder is trained with dense/perception rewards", which is a nuance the field would likely value.

### Cross-angle synthesis (inferences, my assessment)
- **Recommended structure:** headline = readout/arbitration bottleneck. Chapter C (RL moves readout) gives the **training-dynamics** evidence, Chapter B (metacognition) gives the **behavioural/introspective** evidence and a training-free gate, and Chapter A (crowding) gives a **controlled stimulus** with a parametric dial, where the perception-vs-readout split can be read off a psychometric curve.
- **Ranking by novelty × feasibility over 34 days:** B ≈ A > C on scoop safety, and C > B > A on story impact. Compute for all three fits in about 5–6 H200-days (my estimate).
- **Shared infrastructure:** one hidden-state dumping and probing pipeline (per-layer image-token and answer-position states) serves all three. Crowding stimuli can double as the perception-limited items for B, and the RL pairs can be scored on A and B, to test whether RL changes crowding thresholds or metacognitive efficiency.
- **Honest risk:** A and B could both show *encoder-level* failure (probes also fail), which would contradict the headline. Pre-register this as a boundary condition, framed as "we can tell when it IS perception", so the paper survives either outcome.
